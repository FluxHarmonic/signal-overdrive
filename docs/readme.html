Signal Overdrive (feel prototype)

An F-Zero style racer in the Crash The Stack world, built to answer one question: does it feel good enough to become a game? You're Zero, a data courier. Your cargo's integrity is the energy meter: boosting spends it, walls cost it, the checksum lane restores it, and at zero the cargo is corrupt and the run is over.

The track is a Mode 7 ground plane (a fragment shader projecting a tiled track toward the horizon), the machines are sprites, and every frame goes through the Flux Harmonic palette and Bayer dither pass.

Play it

  • https://10.11.0.2:8855/ (self-signed certificate) or http://10.11.0.2:8856/ on wg0
  • Native: scripts/dev sigil build, then scripts/dev ./build/dev/bin/signal-overdrive from this directory

What is in it

  • Two tracks: oval, a wide, gentle test track (the default), and loop, curves of every tightness and a hairpin. Both have boost pads, the repair strip (the "checksum lane") and ICE patches.
  • A 3-lap race against five rivals in three machine designs. They follow the racing line, bump, and move across to block you. Live place, a countdown with a start card, and a finish panel with every machine's race time.
  • The integrity meter. Boosts (from lap 2) spend it, walls cost it, and the checksum lane refills it. It drains in white with what you just spent, and pop-ups say why. At zero the cargo is corrupt.
  • ICE patches (orange and red static). Crossing one scrambles your steering for 1.4 s.
  • A sense of speed: the view widens and pulls back as you go faster, with streaks from the horizon and a punch when you boost.
  • Placeholder sound, synthesized: an engine that follows your speed, a boost whoosh, a wall scrape. On the web it starts at your first key or tap.
  • The best lap's ghost, see-through, from your second lap on (per track, for the session).
  • The uplink gate over the start line.

Screenshots and a short recording are in docs/shots/, and on the hosted site under /shots/.

On a phone the machine accelerates by itself. Three schemes, picked with the SCHEME button (top left) or ?touch=zones|drag|stick:

  • zones (the default): a thumb on the left half steers left, on the right half steers right; both thumbs brake; the BOOST button boosts.
  • drag: one finger anywhere steers by how far it has slid sideways from where it landed (60 px is full lock); a second finger boosts as it lands and drifts while held.
  • stick: a floating stick under the left thumb (sideways steers, pull down to brake); a thumb landing on the right half boosts, and drifts while held.

Reading F3 while you drive

F3 (or ?fps) shows three lines and a graph at the bottom:

  • 60 FPS 16.7 MS SIM 1.2 DRAW 2.4: frames a second, the frame's interval, and the CPU's share in ms: SIM is the race, DRAW is building the frame. A slow frame with small SIM and DRAW is the GPU's.
  • MAX 34MS HITCH 3 AUD 1.8MS F4 LOG: the longest frame in the last three seconds, how many frames have run over 33 ms (a hitch), and the sound's own ms a frame. F4 prints every hitch with its SIM, DRAW, AUDIO and garbage collections to the browser console.
  • SAB 250MS UR 0 CUE WORKLET 0: the audio stream's mode (SAB is the best: off the main thread with shared memory; MSG works; SPROC is on the main thread, the worst), the queue, UR the underruns (times the device ran dry: each is a hitch you hear), and the cue mode with its lag.
  • The graph: one bar a frame for the last 180 frames; the dim line is 16.7 ms (60 fps), the red line 33 ms; red bars are hitches.

If the sound hitches, the numbers that say why: UR rising means the queue ran dry (a deeper ?audiodepth= will help); red bars with a large SIM, DRAW or AUD point at the game; red bars with small ones point at the GPU or the machine being busy.

Tuning

Tab opens the overlay: Up/Down pick a knob, Left/Right change it (hold to repeat), 0 resets it. The overlay's last line is the query string for the current values, and every knob takes a URL parameter of the same name, so "grip 8.5 felt right" is ?grip=8.5. Natively, the same as --grip=8.5. Knobs that change the race (track, laps, rivals, rival pace) restart it on the grid.

Other parameters: ?track=oval|loop (the same as track=0|1), ?mute, ?audiodepth=MS (the engine sound's queue, default 250), ?cues=0 (the one-shot sounds in the stream instead of as cues, the old way), ?hitchlog (every frame over 33 ms to the console), ?touch=zones|drag|stick, ?autopilot (the player drives the racing line), ?start (skip the title), ?fps (the F3 readout from the start).

How it's built

  • src/signal/mode7/ the Mode 7 camera and renderer (nothing racing-specific; the seed of a later substratic-mode7 package)
  • src/signal/handling.sgl one machine's handling, pure, with tests
  • src/signal/track.sgl where a point is on a track; track/oval-data.sgl and track/loop-data.sgl are generated
  • src/signal/race.sgl the grid, laps, rivals, the finish and corrupt cargo
  • src/signal/scene.sgl the frame: the Mode 7 pass, the dither pass, the HUD
  • src/signal/lut.sgl the dither pass by lookup texture (scripts/art/gen-lut.mjs); dither.sgl is Phantom Burn's exact pass
  • src/signal/sound.sgl the synthesized sound; shell/audio.sgl pushes it to the device
  • scripts/touch-check.mjs drives the touch schemes in a headless phone viewport; scripts/shot-web.mjs takes web screenshots; scripts/host serves the build on wg0
  • scripts/art/ the generators for every image in assets/signal/ (node, no dependencies)

Tests: scripts/dev sigil test --sgl test/test-handling.sgl (and test-track, test-camera, test-race, test-sound).
