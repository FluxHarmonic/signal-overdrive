# Conductor and song interface

The game owns song time in simulation ticks. `(signal music conductor)`
computes rows directly from ticks and records section landings as
`#(tick absolute-bar mark)`, newest first. The audible clock and pacing
policy are separate in `(signal music clock)`; the shell applies the
nudge before feeding Substratic's fixed clock.

The race state carries its conductor. Starting or restarting a delivery
resets the song to grid; GO and lap1 start together after four complete
bars. The count-in occupies the last three beats of grid. Pure distance
and forward-speed predictions commit lap2, hold, fill and uplink changes
at least a bar ahead. Fill begins two bars before the predicted final-lap
line and remains committed if the player crashes afterward. Race phases
continue to follow the player's actual crossings.

The stable read interface for beat-linked world effects is:

| Read | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `(race-song race)` | Immutable conductor after the current simulation tick. |
| `(conductor-tick c)` | Absolute song tick at 60 ticks/second; freezes on pause. |
| `(conductor-section c)` | Current mark symbol, including `grid` and `results`. |
| `(conductor-bar c)` | Absolute bar since song reset, starting at zero. |
| `(conductor-row c)` | Row within the current bar, 0 through 31. |
| `(conductor-beat c)` | Beat within the current bar, 0 through 3. |
| `(conductor-position c)` | Tune `(order . row)`, with section-relative loops applied. |
| `(conductor-onset-mask c)` | One-tick TRIGGER pulse; channel 1 is bit 0, channel 8 bit 7. |

Read onset pulses in each simulation tick, after advancing the race. A
frame can contain several ticks; reading only the final conductor can
miss an earlier pulse. World effects should not read the audio player,
sink depth, intended jump log or wall time to determine musical position.
Drawing interpolation remains a later step.

Beat and bar events use successive conductors **within the tick loop**:
a changed `conductor-bar` is a bar event, and a changed bar or beat is a
beat event. Row zero of a new bar is its downbeat. This observes musical
boundaries even on rows with no TRIGGER; the onset mask separately drives
channel-specific effects. Reset observers when the song tick decreases
or a new race starts. Effects already receive a tick-by-tick pulse through
`(signal fx beat)`; its stub can be replaced from these reads without
depending on the audio adapter.

The title integration can observe `race-phase` transitions on the same
race state: entering `title` means title-enter, leaving it for `countdown`
means title-exit/race-start, and `countdown` to `race` is GO. `race-new`
starts `calm`; `race-start` starts `grid` at song tick zero. Enter/Start
or `core-tap!` requests the existing start path. These are observation
seams for the forthcoming title hooks, not newly introduced callback APIs.
Pause freezes the song; a returning focus frame discards its hidden
interval. Keep title animation and effects reads independent of the sink.

`scripts/compile-score` runs Motif's `compile-tune` before the package's
runtime modules compile. It generates `src/signal/music/score.sgl`.
Runtime modules read this data and never compile a tune during a race.
The checked-in diagnostic tune is generated by
`node scripts/music/placeholder.mjs`, under `music/placeholder/`.

The song contract is 140 BPM, speed 3, eight channels and meter `(8 4)`:
32 rows per bar. Patterns can contain multiple whole bars. Marks are
`calm`, `grid`, `lap1`, `lap2`, `hold`, `fill`, `traced`, `uplink`,
and `results`. Tracker `Bxx`, `Dxx` and `Fxx` are rejected: transport
and constant tempo belong to the conductor.

The cue sheet is a data expression, read without evaluation:

```scheme
((id . carrier)
 (engine-top-hz . 220.0)
 (engine-boost-hz . 293.664767917)
 (sections . (
   (calm (loop . (0 . 8)) (next . #f))
   (grid (loop . #f) (next . lap1))
   (lap1 (loop . (8 . 16)) (next . #f))
   (lap2 (loop . (0 . 8)) (next . #f))
   (hold (loop . (4 . 5)) (next . #f))
   (fill (loop . #f) (next . traced))
   (traced (loop . (8 . 16)) (next . #f))
   (uplink (loop . #f) (next . results))
   (results (loop . (0 . 8)) (next . #f)))))
```

Loops are half-open, section-relative bars. Their boundaries must also
be order boundaries because Motif regions loop between order positions.
Grid has four bars; fill has two. The compiler derives section starts
and lengths from the tune's marks and order list.

Generated onsets are `#(absolute-score-row channel midi compiler-sample)`;
channels are 1 through 8 and samples use a 44,100 Hz compilation clock.
They come from Motif's TRIGGER flag. Portamento, vibrato, arpeggios and
note-offs do not create onsets. Per-order row vectors also hold channel
bit masks for constant-time world lookups. The conductor offers a mask
once when a row starts, instead of once per simulation tick.

Carrier can replace the input files without changing clock code:

```sh
CONDUCTOR_TUNE=music/carrier/race.cts \
CONDUCTOR_CUES=music/carrier/cues.sgl scripts/compile-score
```

Those Carrier filenames are illustrative; the composer owns its files.
The source-only compiler reads the locked
Motif and sigil-dsp dependency trees, configurable through `CONDUCTOR_MOTIF`
and `CONDUCTOR_DSP`. `CONDUCTOR_SIGIL` defaults to the installed 0.22.11
compiler matching the game lock, with its cache in `/tmp` and nice 15.

`scripts/test-conductor` checks the clock, loops, decisions and pacing.
`scripts/test-score` checks compiled triggers and multi-bar patterns.
The dev build also compiles the build-time compiler into its module
library directory, so ordinary `sigil test` discovery loads the same
score checks through the native dependency harness. Release/web skip
that extra test-support module.
The captured-audio drift gate remains separate work; these source tests
do not establish audio synchronization.

`(signal music playback)` owns the Motif pull path shared by the live
stream and offline reference. It waits until the actual walker timeline
has passed the preceding bar's first row before handing over a jump.
Its diagnostics record handoff and actual landing frames separately from
the simulation's intended jump log. `test/test-playback.sgl` renders this
path offline through seven channel inserts; it opens no audio device.

`(signal shell music)` adapts this pull path to a separate 250 ms stream,
rendered at the device's rate. It receives the engine/cue context through
`audio-context`; it never opens a device itself. Delivered-frame accounting
uses the sink's accepted count, and the audible clock subtracts queue
depth and reported device latency (35 ms natively). The pure transport
plan joins late audio on the next bar, including any intervening section
landing, with silence before that bar. Pause discards the queue; resume
uses a fresh stream epoch at the frozen simulation tick. Restart also
discards the old queue, including a restart at the same tick.
An explicit shell boot uses `music-reset!` to close and forget the old
adapter context. Ordinary close retains context for pause/resume; a
re-init must bind the current engine context through audio startup and
cannot reopen a previous session's stream behind a muted shell.
Initial PCM is prepared in 256-frame pulls before a stream is opened,
then published in one accepted push. A fresh tick-zero stream discards
its initialization interval and fixed-clock remainder, so setup time is
not counted as progress in the new song. Native drawing is warmed once
at zero dt before audio starts. Subsequent audio service runs before
drawing; resync projection includes the fixed clock's fractional tick
and elapsed work since the conductor's monotonic frame-start timestamp.
This fraction is used for audio timing only, without drawing interpolation.

`test/test-music-sink.sgl` exercises the actual native ring with the
sink's drain shim, asserting that no audio device initialized. The
core uses the adapter alongside the engine/cue stream. F5 pauses both
audio paths and simulation ticks. Web blur/visibility changes also pause,
and the first returning frame discards its hidden interval. The page
reports WebAudio output+base latency every 500 ms. The native shell polls
`(sigil desktop focus)` after each event poll and clears held keys while
unfocused. Its small C addition queries GLFW's current application window;
it creates no window or audio device and can move into the shared desktop
library independently.

When the clock requests resync, the music pull splits at an exact bar
sample already in the walker's timeline. A pending Motif mark lands first,
because `player-jump!` retains pending marks. The seek uses the conductor
projected to the queue's future audible time, rounded to the nearest
tracker row, and restores the cue loop. The simulation never changes.
The stream's clock epoch changes only when the corrected sample reaches
the ear, after queue depth and device latency. Resyncs execute at most
once per simulation bar; diagnostics distinguish requests and completions.
`test/test-resync.sgl` checks exact pull splitting, pending-mark safety,
later planned landings and delayed audible epochs without a device.

`core-advance!` is the same input/pacing/tick path used by each graphical
frame, exposed without drawing for headless checks. The muted
`test/test-shell-clock.sgl` checks wall pacing, the six-tick stall cap,
focus pause and explicit pause across focus changes.
`--music-headless` uses that path plus the shared audio/input/trace frame
tail, with real monotonic intervals and bounded sleeps. It runs an actual
autopilot race without drawing or a window to isolate graphics contention.
Captured runs record the selected mode; graphical results remain separate.

`--musiclog` (or `?musiclog`) prints simulation intended landings and
dated clock/cost samples. The clock timestamp is taken before drawing
and rendering, so CPU time is not mistaken for clock drift. Intended
landings are independent of the player's actual landing diagnostics.
The gate renders its reference from the intended log.
The native `--music-reference PLAN PCM FRAMES [RATE]` command uses
`(signal music reference)` and exits before shell/audio/window boot. It
asserts that audio remains uninitialized; RATE defaults to 48 kHz for
existing callers. Actual captures render at the device's logged source
rate, recorded in metadata, and analyze against the 48 kHz monitor using
envelopes at a common 3 kHz clock. `scripts/drift/build-candidate`
builds the release and checks grid→lap1 at both 48 and 44.1 kHz; the capture
driver invokes it for each actual intended race log. All gate tools
and tests are committed under `scripts/drift/`; no temporary copy is
needed to reproduce them. Music cost is sampled on every traced frame.

The three diagnostic arms are `--music-sabotage=depth` (omit queue depth
from the audible reading), `immediate` (hand over planned marks at once)
and `skew` (plant 1% in the shell clock and disable its nudge). `none`
restores the normal path. Resync remains active, so each arm tests the
actual system including its recovery behavior.

### Known native music underruns

Leader 21:40 (2026-10-13) authorizes local integration with this issue
recorded for the next audio/drift worker. The final native graphical
race on runtime commit `3d0fbc9` completed all eight intended sections,
the results coda and a clean Escape exit, but failed zero sounding
music underruns: **8,414 frames at 44,100 Hz** (about 191 ms).
Of these, **7,407 frames** (about 168 ms) were already present at title
startup; **1,007 more** (about 23 ms) first appeared at tick 6,119,
song time 101.983 s, phase `finished`, before the planned results landing.
The engine remained at zero sounding underruns. The final web race on
hardware AMD Radeon 780M/RADV had zero engine and music underruns.

Conditions: private Xvfb, scale 2, software GL/llvmpipe with one rendering
thread, CPU 5 only, nice 15, 250 ms music ring, PulseAudio forced to the
verified `worker-null` sink (128), native device rate 44,100 Hz, and a
machine at load roughly 11–14. No speaker or foreground display was used.
Evidence remains in this conductor worktree under
`build/conductor-final-native-race-retry/`: `RESULT`, `game.log`,
`routing.json` and `verification-failed.json`. The exact corrected
verification helper is `build/conductor-final-native-race-check.mjs`;
its log is `build/conductor-final-native-race-retry-helper.log`.

A pristine `9ac977c` master comparison used the same helper/environment.
Its engine counter was zero at title startup and all six periodic samples
through lap 3; the race finished, but Escape exited 139 (the older graphics
teardown defect fixed by this branch). Master has no music stream, so this
is not a comparison of the same music lane. No post-finish engine counter
was emitted before its four-second coda/quit. Evidence is under
`build/conductor-master-native-race/` (`comparison.json`, `launch.json`,
`routing.json`, `received.json`, `game.log` and `RESULT`). Its comparison
PASS means collection completed, not clean shutdown or a music-zero claim.

The hypothesis is that main-thread presentation, allocation or collector
stalls drain the new music lane at startup and near finish. Graphics warmup
and pre-rendered initial PCM already precede music publication; the exact
remaining cause has not been isolated. These native underruns and the
separate known-RED drift gate pass to a fresh worker, who should start on
real hardware to distinguish the environment from the product. Neither
failure is hidden by a counter reset or weaker gate thresholds.
